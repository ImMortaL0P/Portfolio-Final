Why I Call Creativity a Process, and Its Hidden Toll

Personal Essay Jul 18, 2026 Written By K. Mangalam

[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] Creativity is not a lightning bolt. It is not the romantic, effortless spark that people love to imagine—a sudden strike of genius that arrives fully formed. It is a process. A slow, grinding, often unglamorous process that demands more from you than talent ever could.

Over the years of designing, photographing, and building things from nothing, I've come to understand that the act of creating is as much about endurance as it is about inspiration. And that endurance comes at a cost no one really talks about.

The space between idea and execution is where the real work lives.

What do you mean when you say creativity is a process? [PLACEHOLDER TEXT] People see the finished poster, the final photograph, the polished reel, and they assume it appeared. But behind every piece is a trail of discarded drafts, 3 AM revisions, and moments where I genuinely questioned whether the idea was worth pursuing at all. The process is iteration. It's sitting with discomfort. It's trusting that the ugly first draft is a necessary step, not a failure.

What is the "hidden toll" you refer to? [PLACEHOLDER TEXT] The toll is what you pay when the work demands your full emotional presence — when you pour yourself into a design and someone dismisses it in two seconds, when you spend hours perfecting a frame and the light shifts, when you ship something personal and the silence that follows feels deafening. The toll is also physical: skipped meals, strained eyes, the weight of self-imposed deadlines. Nobody warns you that caring deeply about your craft can feel like carrying a stone uphill.

How do you keep going when the process feels overwhelming? [PLACEHOLDER TEXT] I've learned to separate my identity from my output. A bad design day doesn't make me a bad designer. A rejected pitch doesn't erase everything I've built. I keep going because the process — however draining — is also where I feel most alive. There's a strange paradox in creative work: the thing that exhausts you is also the thing that gives you meaning.

[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] "If you're someone who creates—whether it's code, photographs, music, or words—know that the exhaustion you feel is not weakness. It's proof that you're doing something real. The toll is hidden because we've been taught to only celebrate the output. But the process? The process is where the art actually lives."
