What I Learned from the Streets of Patna

Visual Story Essay Sept 12, 2023 Written By K. Mangalam

[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] I stumbled upon the bustling chaotic markets of Patna one day deep in a photography rabbit hole, and I’m pretty sure I audibly gasped the first time I saw the sheer raw energy of it. The streets are a tapestry of history, daily struggle, and vibrant, unscripted poetry.

Fast forward a few months when I traveled there with my camera, I knew I had to document it! Excited to share these visual placeholders from the streets, and some thoughts on what observing this chaos taught me.

Shot on a mix of 35mm & medium format film.
What draws you to chaotic environments like these?
[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] "There is an unspoken rhythm to the way the traffic weaves, the way the street vendors carve out their space, and the way people navigate the narrow lanes. It taught me that chaos isn't always disorder; sometimes, it's just an order that we haven't yet learned to understand."
How does one photograph a place that never stops moving?
[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] "Street photography here cannot be rushed. You don't hunt for moments; you wait for them to assemble themselves. You wait for the right character to enter the stage. You wait for the glance, the gesture, the fleeting alignment of light and shadow."
Do you often interact with your subjects before clicking?
[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] "In many parts of the world, raising a camera on the street invites suspicion. Here, it often invites curiosity and conversation. People want to know who you are, why you are taking photos, and sometimes, they insist you take their portrait. This forced me to lower the camera and engage."

[PLACEHOLDER TEXT] "The tangle of overhead wires, the peeling posters, the crowded backgrounds—these are not distractions; they are the context. Learning to embrace this visual complexity taught me a broader lesson: perfection is often boring, but authenticity is always compelling."
