25 Years on This Planet: Lessons Taught by Time

Photo Journal Jul 18, 2026 By K. Mangalam
Play Me!
Viva La Vida x Pompeii Paused

I will be turning Twenty-five this year and Twenty-five is not old. But it is old enough to have been wrong about important things, to have lost people you thought would stay, and to have built something from the wreckage of plans that didn't survive contact with reality. This is the journal of my failures as a 25 year old and the lessons that are still shaping my future

01

ITS OK TO DOUBT YOURSELF

A lot will go wrong before even one thing goes right, and its okay. Doubting your decisions is not a sign of impending doom but a strong indicator that you care about the outcome. And even if the outcome is not what you wanted, what did you lose really? There's a far greater learning in failures that there is in successes. Recent 3-4 years I have taken decisions that have made me second guess myself and my potential, resigning from my job, preapring for an alien CAT exam sit on the epitome of it. I was scared to the point of giving up, not going for exams or even rejoining my work, but looking back, it all did workout at the end.

02

Your taste will always outrun your skill

You can see the gap. Between what you make and what you know is possible. Between the design in your head and the one on the screen. This gap is not a flaw—it is proof that your standards are higher than your current ability, and that is exactly the tension that drives growth. The danger is not in having the gap; it is in letting it convince you to stop. Whenever I sit to design a new piece, I ingest so many contents to draw inspiration from, but I always end up with a design that is not even close to what I imagined. But the design I still end up with is better than what I could have made a year ago. Treat this as a compass pointing you toward the next level.

03

The family you choose for yourself.

Your Friends or Homies whatever you call them. They are essentially the family you choose for yourself. They will be around you in your highs dmenading treats and in your lows, laughing at you first but none the less consoling you. I did a mistake a few years back pushing them away at my lowest, and damn if I knew then what I know now. No matter what you are going through, these people will have either one of the two things for you, a solution or a place to sit and talk. They might not be in your touch on a daily basis, but they will be in you touch at any moment you need. Love them, cherish them, laugh with them and dont forget to laugh on them!

04

Your Biggest Strength.

The family you are born into. Your mom may not always hold you like she used to do when you were little, you dad may not always prize you like he used to do, your siblings may not say I love you so often now, but they will be the ones you will find behidn you at all steps. There will be times when you will feel like they dont understand you, they dont see the wold like you do, but remember they are also experiencing this for the first time like you, and unlike you they often dont have the luxury to fall back to their family for decisions that they find difficult to make on their own. Love them, as they love you, support them as they support you. I cant be gratefl enough for my family, for making me who I am, and I am sure you will feel the same.

05

Heart Break Syndrome

Falling in love is easier than staying in love, your partners outgrow you. The strong ones will move on easily, but the attached ones will keep searching for answers, what they could have done differently to still have them in their life. But the truth is, those answers dont exist, and I know its difficult to accept this and move on with your life, especially when you planned your whole life around them, that just feels like a void. But you need to allow new people to come in your life, experience life again, and remember that chapter as an essential part of your life that taught you to grow, and see the world in a different light, without them. One heartbreak wont define how you feel about life, but it will define how strong you can be.

06

Academics are important, skills are more important.

As soon as I completed my B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering, I joined JSPL. Assuming I will be working in my own domain I was ecstatic, but soon I received the call that I will be posted in Steel Casters. A metallurgy heavy space, I thought I will be struggling to adapt, but thats exactly when my skills came in handy. Being a quick learner, I quicky picked up the basics and started taking up small responsibilities, eventually taking on the big ones. My skills earned me a place in that competetive environment, earned two prestigous awards and a name in the entire department. Skills will take you places where your degree can just get you through the door.

"Twenty-five years is not a milestone. It is a checkpoint. A place to pause, look at the map, and acknowledge that you've been walking in roughly the right direction—even when it didn't feel like it. The lessons here are not finished. They are still being written, one frame at a time."
